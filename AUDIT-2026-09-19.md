Short version: the numbers on screen right now are not lies about the data, they are honest math over the wrong data. The events table is empty (zero Pulse events on any site), so every number is GA4 history for 3 sites, and the GA4 lead mapping counts things that are not leads. Fix the inputs and the definitions first, then the pages start making sense.

## Bugs that make numbers wrong (fix these first)

### 1. `form_start` is counted as a form lead
`lib/google.js:131`, the forms regex is `/form|generate_lead|.../`. GA4 enhanced measurement fires `form_start` the moment anyone clicks into a field. It also matches `form_error`, `form_view`, anything. Result: 4 of the 5 "forms" in the database never happened. Fix: exact allowlist, not regex. `form_submit`, `lead_form_submit`, `generate_lead`, `contact_form_submit`. Never anything with `start`, `view`, `error`, `impression`.

### 2. GA4 cannot see phone calls, so "calls: 0" is a blind spot, not a fact
GA4 does not track `tel:` clicks unless someone set up a custom event. CHM has 323 users and 0 calls in GA4 because nothing was ever measuring calls there, not because nobody called. Same for Simply Salt. Only Green Gate had `call_click` because Wix adds it. Until the Pulse snippet is on a site, its call count is unknown, and the UI should say "unknown", not 0.

### 3. Leads are raw clicks, not people
`lib/stats.js:263` and every Portfolio query use `count(*)` on conversion events. One visitor tapping the phone number three times is 3 leads. A form that fails validation and gets resubmitted is 2 leads. `summary()` already computes `converting_sessions` (line 40) but nothing shows it. A lead should be one session, one type, one day: `count(distinct (session_id, type))`.

### 4. The "Leads after launch" curve drops, which a running total cannot do
`lib/stats.js:319-328`. Day d averages only sites live at least d days. Green Gate (38 days live) is in the average through day 37 and vanishes on day 38, so the line falls from 3 to 1. The tiles above the chart only count complete sites (`Portfolio.js:37`), so the chart and the tiles are computed over different populations. Fix: average the same complete set as the tiles, and draw every site as its own thin line so the average is visibly an average of something.

### 5. "Day 0" is invented when no launch date is set
`lib/stats.js:282`: `coalesce(launched_at, first day with any data)`. CHM's site is over a year old but GA4 was installed 06-27, so Pulse says CHM launched 06-27 and "got its first lead on day 31". Fix: no launch date means excluded from benchmarks with a loud "set launch date" badge. Never guess a launch date from data.

### 6. Proof lines write themselves off one site
`Portfolio.js:61-70`. n=1 produced "100% of sites got a lead inside 60 days" and "never later than day 31". Minimum n=3 before a line is written, and print n in every line. The page note already says "only once 5+ sites", the code does not enforce it.

### 7. The per-site pages ignore GA4 history entirely
Overview, Pages, Conversions, Sources (`app/api/stats/route.js:30-55`) read only `events`. Portfolio reads the merged `daystats`. So today Portfolio says CHM had 323 visitors and Overview for CHM says nothing. Same site, two answers. The daily chart and the visitor tiles on Overview should use the same merge, with a "GA4 history" marker on those days.

### 8. Day boundaries use two different clocks
`DAYSTATS` (`lib/stats.js:259`) buckets by `created_at::date`, which is UTC. Every per-site query buckets by the site's timezone (`lib/stats.js:21`). A 9pm call in Florida lands on tomorrow in Portfolio and today in Overview. Use `(created_at at time zone s.timezone)::date` everywhere.

### 9. Portfolio "visitors" double-counts across days
`ga_daily.users` summed over days counts a returning visitor once per day. Label it "daily visitors" or use sessions for the denominator. Conversion rate against an inflated denominator reads low.

## Holes in the tracking itself (the snippet and collect endpoint)

### 10. Nothing is being tracked, on any site, as of tonight
`pulse.ryderschilling.com` is not attached to the Vercel project, so every installed snippet 404s. Two more sites have no snippet in a live repo (CHM, Green Gate). This is the single biggest reason the platform reads empty. Attach the domain, push the three tagged repos, add the tag to the CHM repo and the Wix custom code.

### 11. Green Gate went dark on 09-06 and nothing flagged it
GA4 has zero rows for Green Gate after 09-06. Bryan moved back to Wix on 09-10, and either the GA4 tag is not on the Wix site or it is a different property. The platform needs a per-site health status (last hit, expected vs actual, "no data for N days" warning at the top of every page), otherwise a dead tag looks like a slow month.

### 12. Preview deploys, localhost and your own visits count as traffic
`app/api/collect/route.js:37` only checks that the site key exists. The snippet sends no hostname. So `*.vercel.app` previews, `localhost:3000`, and Cursor's browser preview all post real-looking pageviews to the real site. Fix: snippet sends `location.host`; collect drops anything that does not match `sites.domain` (with or without www). Also add an ignore switch: visiting `?pulse_ignore=1` once sets a localStorage flag and that browser is never counted (you, the client, Reece).

### 13. Bot filtering is user-agent only
`collect/route.js:42`. Headless Chrome with a normal UA, uptime pingers with custom UAs, and the scraper wave that makes Simply Salt show 443 sessions with 77 engaged all get through. GA4 has the same problem, which is why 410 "users" in two weeks for a Peoria salt route is not believable. Add an "engaged visitor" definition (2+ pageviews, or a leave event over 10 seconds, or scroll past 25%) and make that the headline visitor number. Raw visitors stay as a secondary.

### 14. Sessions break on new tabs
`public/p.js:41-44`. Session id lives in sessionStorage, which is per tab. A visitor who opens a page in a new tab is a new session and a new bounce. Keep the session id and last-activity timestamp in localStorage with the same 30 minute rule.

### 15. Form tracking counts attempts, not successes
`public/p.js:149-156` fires on the `submit` event. A JS form that blocks on validation and resubmits fires twice. A Next.js form that posts with fetch and never fires `submit` (button onClick) fires zero times. For the Next.js sites (Simply Salt, CHM) the reliable move is `window.pulse('form', {label})` in the success branch after the API returns 200, and the snippet listener stays as the fallback for the static sites.

### 16. Desktop tel: clicks are counted as calls
A desktop tel: click opens FaceTime or nothing. Keep counting it, but split calls by device on the Conversions page, and in the proof lines count mobile calls only, or say "call taps".

### 17. Anyone can post events to any site key
Site keys are public in the page source, and collect has no origin or domain check. A competitor or a bored kid can fill Momentum's numbers with fake leads. The domain check in item 12 closes most of it. Add a per-site daily cap and a rate limit per IP on top.

## Missing pieces for "track sites over time"

### 18. No scheduled sync, so GA4 and GSC history stop whenever you last pressed the button
Backfill is a manual button (`Settings`). GSC is fetched live and cached for an hour, never stored. Add a Vercel cron (`vercel.json`, daily 6am) that pulls GA4 daily rows and GSC daily rows (clicks, impressions, position, top 25 queries) into `ga_daily` and a new `gsc_daily`. Then the Search page can show ranking growth month over month, which is the retainer story.

### 19. No install verification
Settings has no "check this site" button. Fetch the site's homepage server-side, look for `p.js` with the right key, show green/red. Ten seconds to build, saves the "why is it empty" call every time.

### 20. Leads are still clicks, and clicks are the ceiling on accuracy
The 100% accurate version of a lead is not a tel: click. It is a call that connected, and a form whose contents you have. Two upgrades, both sellable:
- Call tracking numbers: a Twilio number per site forwarding to the client's phone. Pulse logs every real call with duration and caller. This is what CallRail charges $45 to $145 a month for. Sits naturally on the $399 retainer.
- Lead webhook: `/api/lead?site=ps_x` that the site's form handler (Resend, FormSubmit, the AKW booking flow) posts to after a successful send. The lead feed then shows the actual name and message, and a form lead means a real form.
Until those exist, label the current numbers "call taps" and "form submits" in the UI.

### 21. No client-facing report
The point of the retainer is proving value monthly. A shareable read-only page per site (`/r/<token>`) with the month's visitors, leads, top pages and top queries, plus a monthly email, turns Pulse from your tool into their reason to keep paying.

### 22. Portfolio needs the same-population rule everywhere
Tiles, chart, by-niche table and proof lines should all be computed over one set: sites with a real launch date, snippet or GA4 data through the whole window, and at least the window's days live. Show the excluded sites in a separate "not counted yet, here is why" list.

## Smaller things noticed
- `ensureSchema` runs a dozen DDL statements on every cold start (`lib/db.js:35`). Harmless, adds latency. Gate it behind an env flag or a version row.
- `previousRange` and the Overview deltas compare against a period that may have no data at all and show "-100%". Show "no prior data" instead.
- `sources()` referrers: `www.` vs apex mismatch in `p.js:144` makes internal links on an apex-only domain read as outbound when the site links to www.
- The Settings snippet text prints `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL`. Confirm it is `https://pulse.ryderschilling.com` on Vercel or every copied snippet points at localhost.
- No index on `events (site_id, visitor_id)`, the visitor counts will slow down past a few million rows. Not urgent.
